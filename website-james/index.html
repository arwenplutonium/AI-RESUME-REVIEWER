BUILD A STRONGER APPLICATION

The CV field guide

A practical guide to presenting your experience with clarity and confidence.

01 / THE FOUNDATION

Make the first impression count.

Your CV should make it easy for a recruiter to understand who you are, what you can do and why you are relevant to the role.

  • Use a clear name and contact section.
  • Write a concise, role-focused profile.
  • Put your most relevant experience where it is easy to find.
02 / EXPERIENCE

Show the impact

Start bullet points with strong action verbs. Explain what you did, how you did it and what changed as a result.

LESS EFFECTIVE Responsible for social media.
MORE EFFECTIVE Planned weekly social posts and improved audience engagement through consistent content.
03 / ATS READINESS

Keep formatting readable

Use standard section headings, simple layouts and relevant keywords from the job description. Avoid placing essential information inside graphics.

✓ Clear section headings
✓ Consistent dates and formatting
✓ Relevant role keywords
04 / FINAL CHECK

Before you apply

Give your CV one final review before sending it.

✓ Check spelling and grammar
✓ Confirm contact details
✓ Match the CV to the vacancy
✓ Save with a professional filename
05 / HOW YOU'RE SCORED

Score bands

Every CV and cover letter is scored out of 100 using a rubric based on Harvard FAS Mignone Center for Career Success guidance.

90–100 · ExcellentPolished and targeted. Only minor refinements needed.
75–89 · GoodSolid, with targeted improvements to wording, evidence or tailoring.
60–74 · DevelopingSeveral important improvements needed, especially evidence and clarity.
Below 60 · Needs major revisionNeeds restructuring, stronger evidence and more relevant content.

Our reviewer will never invent experience, numbers or skills. Only add achievements that are true and that you can explain in an interview.
